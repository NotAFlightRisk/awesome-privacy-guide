<Short>
Start with the swaps that take minutes: [your search engine](#search), [your browser](#browser) and [your maps app](#maps). Then move the services that hold your things, [Drive and Photos](#drive-and-photos) and then [Gmail](#email), one at a time. You don't have to leave everything, and if you keep a Google account, [turn on auto-delete](#keep-it-tidy) for whatever stays in it.
</Short>

## Get a copy of everything first

<Tip id="takeout" priority="essential" effort={15} title="Download your data with Google Takeout">
Google Takeout exports your data from the Google products you use, like your email, documents, calendar, photos and YouTube videos. Downloading it doesn't delete anything from Google's servers.[^takeout] Do one export now, before you start moving things, so whatever happens later, you've got a copy. A big export can take a while to arrive, and Google may delay it if something about the request looks risky.[^takeout]
</Tip>

## The swaps that take minutes

<Tip id="search" priority="essential" effort={2} title="Change your search engine">
The <Swap product="google">Google Search</Swap> page explains what Google does with your searches. Changing the default in your browser takes a couple of minutes, and the [browser privacy guide](/guides/browser-privacy/#search-engine) has the steps and the list's picks.
</Tip>

<Tip id="browser" priority="optional" effort={30} title="Move off Chrome">
The [browser privacy guide](/guides/browser-privacy/#leave-chrome) covers why Chrome is the hardest browser to make private, and which ones to use instead. If Chrome has your passwords, the [password manager guide](/guides/password-manager/) covers getting them out safely first.
</Tip>

<Tip id="maps" priority="optional" effort={10} title="Use another maps app for everyday trips">
When its history setting is on, Google Maps saves the places you search for and the directions you get to your Google account.[^maps-history] The <Swap product="google-maps">Google Maps</Swap> page lists the alternatives. You don't have to delete Google Maps to start. Use the new app for the trips you make every week, and keep Google's for the odd search it does better, signed out, so nothing gets saved to your account.
</Tip>

<Tip id="dns" priority="optional" effort={5} title="Swap Google's DNS, if you've set it">
If you, or whoever set up your router, pointed your devices at Google Public DNS, every site you look up goes past Google. The <Swap product="google-dns">Google DNS</Swap> page lists other providers, and in the browser, <Term slug="encrypted-dns">secure DNS</Term> lets you pick one without touching your router.
</Tip>

## The ones that take an afternoon

<Tip id="small-ones" priority="optional" effort={30} title="Move your notes, calendar and calls">
These are quick to move once you've picked where they're going. The <Swap product="google-keep">Google Keep</Swap>, <Swap product="google-calendar">Google Calendar</Swap> and <Swap product="google-meet">Google Meet</Swap> pages list the alternatives. Your calendar can come across in your Takeout export, and if you're also leaving Gmail, the [leave Gmail guide](/guides/leave-gmail/#contacts-and-calendars) moves contacts and calendars together. For calls, you only need another app for the ones you set up yourself. When someone else sends you a Meet link, joining it is fine.
</Tip>

<Tip id="drive-and-photos" priority="optional" effort={120} title="Move Drive and Photos somewhere Google can't read">
These usually take longest, because there's so much in them. The <Swap product="google-drive">Google Drive</Swap> and <Swap product="google-photos">Google Photos</Swap> pages list where you can move them. Check the price for the space you actually need first, since years of photos can be a lot more than a free plan holds.

Move everything across, then open a few files and albums on the new service to check they made it. Only then delete them from Google. Downloading your data doesn't delete it,[^takeout] and to remove a whole Google service from your account, open your Google Account, go to <Path>Data & privacy</Path>, and under Tools, select Remove data from services you no longer use.[^remove]
</Tip>

## The ones that take months

<Tip id="email" priority="optional" effort={60} title="Leave Gmail last of the big ones">
Email takes longest, because every account you've ever made sends its password resets there. The [leave Gmail guide](/guides/leave-gmail/) does it in order: a new provider, ideally on your own domain, forwarding while you move your accounts across, and keeping Gmail alive for a year.
</Tip>

<Tip id="phone" priority="advanced" effort={120} title="Replace Android itself, if you're ready to">
A phone without Google's services means installing a different operating system, a <Term slug="custom-rom">custom ROM</Term>, and installing one wipes everything on the phone along the way.[^graphene] The <Swap product="android">Android</Swap> page lists the options. <App name="GrapheneOS" /> only supports Google's own Pixel phones, and phones sold through some US carriers can be locked so it can't be installed at all.[^graphene] For other phones, [Android ROM Compat](https://android-rom-compat.peng.ly) shows which systems run on which models. Back up first, and check that the apps you can't do without, like your bank's, work on the system you pick before you move.
</Tip>

## What's fine to keep

YouTube is the hardest to replace, because so much of what you'd want to watch is only there. You can still watch it without a Google account, though. <App name="Invidious" /> shows YouTube's videos without Google's tracking, with subscriptions that don't depend on Google, and it can import the ones you already have on YouTube.[^invidious] The <Swap product="youtube">YouTube</Swap> page lists other video sites. Plenty of people stop there, and that's a sensible place to stop.

You may want to keep your Google account open for a while anyway, so that old accounts still sending resets to it keep working. If nobody signs in for two years, Google can delete the account and everything in it,[^inactive] so sign in now and then while you still need it.

<Tip id="keep-it-tidy" priority="optional" effort={5} title="Turn on auto-delete for what you keep">
For anything that stays in your Google account, open your Google Account, go to <Path>Data & privacy</Path>, and under History settings, pick each history setting, select Auto-delete, and choose how long to keep it.[^delete] Google says it may keep some data longer for business or legal reasons, so auto-delete limits what's kept rather than wiping everything.[^delete] In My Ad Center, you can also turn off personalised ads, so Google stops using your activity to pick the ads you see.[^ads]
</Tip>
